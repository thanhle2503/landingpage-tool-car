Tool.Cars notes: which dash cam fits which driver

How we would choose, based on each brand's published specs and prices.

One camera, keep it simple: the ROVE R2-4K (about $120) records 4K at the front, with WiFi, GPS and a supercapacitor parking mode.

Front and rear: the ROVE R2-4K Dual (about $150) records both together with a Sony STARVIS 2 sensor for night video, and comes with a free 128GB card.

Rideshare and taxi drivers: the ROVE R3 (about $300) adds a cabin camera with IR night vision, so you get front, cabin and rear.

Premium 4K front camera: the Fanttik Luma K10 Apex (about $320).

24/7 parking mode: add the ROVE hardwire kit (about $30) and a fast card such as the ROVE Ultimate 256GB.

Questions, answered

Short answers based on what each brand lists on its product pages.

Which dash cam records front and rear?

The ROVE R2-4K Dual records front and rear at the same time (upscaled 4K front, 1080P rear) and comes with a 128GB card. The ROVE R3 adds a third camera for the cabin, recording 1440P front plus 1080P cabin and rear.

ROVE or Fanttik: which brand should I pick?

ROVE has the wider range here, from a single 4K front camera to 3-channel recording, plus its own mounts, filters, hardwire kits and microSD cards. Fanttik has one dash cam on this page, the Luma K10 Apex 4K, sold on fanttik.com.

Do I need a hardwire kit?

Only for parking mode. A hardwire kit connects the dash cam to the fuse box so it can keep recording while the car is parked. The ROVE kit fits the R2-4K (USB-C), R2-4K PRO, R2-4K Dual and R3.

What memory card should I use in a dash cam?

Use a high-endurance card rated U3 / V30, since a dash cam writes video all the time. ROVE sells 128GB, 256GB and 1TB microSD cards made for its 4K cameras.
