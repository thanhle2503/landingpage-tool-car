MA900 questions, answered

Short answers based on the official iCARZONE product page.

Is the iCARZONE MA900 worth it?

For most car owners, yes. At about $70 the MA900 replaces two tools: a full OBD2 code reader that clears the check engine light, and a 12V/24V battery, cranking and charging tester. It has lifetime free updates and no subscription. It is not a bidirectional tool and does not read ABS or airbag codes.

Can the MA900 read ABS and airbag (SRS) codes?

No. The MA900 reads engine (powertrain) codes and all 10 OBD2 modes, including live data, freeze frame, O2 sensor, EVAP and I/M readiness. For ABS and SRS codes, look at the iCARZONE MA200 Plus or the UR600, which also add bidirectional control and service resets.

What battery tests does the MA900 do?

It checks battery state of health (SOH), state of charge (SOC), internal resistance and rated CCA on 12V and 24V systems, then tests starter cranking voltage and alternator charging output. The result says in plain words whether the battery is good, needs charging or should be replaced.

Which cars does the MA900 work with?

All OBD2/EOBD vehicles: 1996 and newer cars sold in the US, 2001+ cars in the EU and 2008+ cars in China. The battery tester works on 12V cars and 24V commercial vehicles such as trucks and RVs. It plugs straight into the OBD2 port with no app or phone needed.

MA900 vs MA500: what is the difference?

The MA500 (from about $33) is a battery and charging system tester only. The MA900 (about $70) does the same battery, cranking and charging tests and adds a full OBD2 code reader. If you also want to read and clear check engine codes, the MA900 is the better buy.
